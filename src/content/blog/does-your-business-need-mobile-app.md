"We need an app."

It's one of the most common requests we hear. And in most cases, the honest answer is: no, you probably don't.

Mobile apps are expensive to build, expensive to maintain, and require users to actually download and keep them. Before investing $1,000-35,000 in app development, let's figure out if it's really the right solution.

## When You DON'T Need an App

### Your Website Works Fine on Mobile

If customers just need to browse products, read information, or fill out forms, a well-designed mobile website does this perfectly. No download required, no app store approval, no updates to push.

**Use a mobile website when:**
- Content consumption is the main use case
- Transactions are infrequent (monthly or less)
- Users don't need offline access
- You're on a limited budget

### You Want "An App" for Marketing Reasons

An app in the app store isn't a marketing strategy. Users don't browse app stores looking for businesses to patronize. They download apps to solve specific problems.

If your app doesn't offer something users genuinely need multiple times per week, it'll be downloaded once and forgotten — or deleted to free up storage.

### Your Customers Wouldn't Use It Regularly

Apps make sense when users engage frequently. If your typical customer interacts with you once a month or less, they won't keep your app installed.

**The brutal truth:** Most business apps have terrible retention. 25% of apps are used once and never opened again. Within 90 days, 70%+ of users have abandoned most apps.

### A Web App Solves the Same Problem

Progressive Web Apps (PWAs) can now do most things native apps can:
- Work offline
- Send push notifications
- Access camera and GPS
- Add icon to home screen

PWAs cost significantly less than native apps and work across all devices. They're often the smarter choice.

## When You DO Need an App

### Heavy Offline Functionality

If users need full functionality without internet — field workers, delivery drivers, areas with poor connectivity — native apps handle offline data better than web alternatives.

### Intensive Device Features

Some hardware access is still better on native:
- Advanced camera features
- Bluetooth device connections
- Background GPS tracking
- Complex sensor data

### High-Frequency Engagement

If users interact with your service daily or multiple times per day, an app's speed and convenience make sense:
- Banking and payments
- Communication tools
- Fitness tracking
- Daily task management

### Brand Experience and Loyalty

For some businesses, the app IS the product experience. If you're building a consumer-facing service where the app defines the brand, native development makes sense.

## Questions to Ask Before Building

### 1. How Often Will Users Open This?

Be honest. If the answer is "a few times a month," an app probably isn't justified. Apps need regular engagement to stay installed and top-of-mind.

### 2. What Can an App Do That a Website Can't?

List the specific features requiring native app capabilities. If the list is short or theoretical, reconsider.

### 3. What's the Total Budget — Including Maintenance?

Building the app is 40% of the cost. Ongoing maintenance, updates for new OS versions, app store management, and feature additions are the rest.

**Year-one costs might break down:**
- Initial development: $5,000
- Bug fixes and updates: $1,000
- Feature additions: $2,000
- App store fees and management: $200
- **Total year one: $8,200**

And that's every year going forward, minus the initial development.

### 4. iOS and Android — Or Just One?

Supporting both platforms roughly doubles development and maintenance costs. Can you launch on one first and validate demand?

### 5. Will Users Actually Download It?

Getting app downloads is hard and expensive. Do you have:
- A large, engaged existing audience?
- Marketing budget for app promotion?
- A compelling reason for users to download?

### 6. What's the Alternative?

Before committing to app development, explore:
- Mobile-optimized website
- Progressive Web App
- Integration with existing apps (WhatsApp Business, etc.)
- Third-party platforms with existing user bases

## The Real Costs of Mobile Apps

### Development Costs

| App Complexity | iOS Only | Both Platforms |
|----------------|----------|----------------|
| Simple app | $1,000-3,000 | $1,500-5,000 |
| Medium complexity | $3,000-8,000 | $5,000-12,000 |
| Complex app | $8,000-20,000 | $12,000-35,000 |

### Ongoing Costs (Annual)

- Bug fixes and maintenance: 15-20% of initial development
- OS compatibility updates: $5,000-15,000/year
- Feature enhancements: Varies based on roadmap
- App store fees: $100 (Apple) + $25 (Google) annually
- Server/backend costs: $200-2,000+/month

### Hidden Costs

- App store approval process and potential rejections
- Negative reviews impacting brand perception
- Supporting multiple OS versions
- Device fragmentation testing
- User acquisition marketing

## A Decision Framework

**Build a native app if:**
- ✅ Users will engage daily or multiple times weekly
- ✅ Core features require native device capabilities
- ✅ Offline functionality is essential
- ✅ You have budget for development AND ongoing maintenance
- ✅ You have a strategy to acquire and retain users

**Build a PWA or mobile website if:**
- ✅ Engagement is periodic (weekly or less)
- ✅ Features work fine via web
- ✅ Budget is limited
- ✅ Speed to market is important
- ✅ You want to test demand before major investment

**Don't build anything if:**
- ❌ "Having an app" is the goal, not solving a user problem
- ❌ You can't articulate why users would download it
- ❌ There's no budget for ongoing maintenance
- ❌ Your existing customers aren't asking for it
